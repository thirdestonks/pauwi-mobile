How it plays

  • Where: at most one per house, in about 2 flats in 5 and 1 storeroom in 7. Only in drawers, wardrobes, beds, filing cabinets, boxes, backpacks, trunks and biscuit tins.
  • No bag slot. Like the bedroom key, a keepsake goes into Juan's jacket, never the 10-slot bag.
  • Reading it the first time: +3 Hope. Juan says one line about it in the message box.
  • Tip notes (3 of the 12) mark a supply cache on the phone map, the same ? ring a radio tip makes, on the street the note names.
  • Photos turn over: tap one to read the back.
  • At the shelter Juan pins what he carried on the wall. In Nida's store that's the counter front, where papers were already pinned. Tap it for the close-up wall; tap any keepsake to read it again (no Hope the second time).
  • The wall survives death. These are memories. A new run rolls keepsakes you don't have yet first.

Stand-ins

  • 8 photos: code-drawn scenes until the two photo sheets are painted.
  • 8 paper kinds (notebook, sticky, crayon drawing, calendar, pawn ticket, letter, prayer card, napkin): code-drawn until the paper sheet is painted. The writing is always drawn in code.
  • The close-up wall is code-drawn for good (the user's call): a cork board on planks, lit by a kerosene lantern off the bottom left.
  • Handwriting uses a system font here; the app will bundle one so Android looks the same.
