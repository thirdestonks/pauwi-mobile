01 — Why

Combat is still the vanilla-JS demo ported from reference.html — the file says so in its own header comment. Every value is a local ref that dies on unmount, so a fight has no memory, no stakes, and no way to lose. This pass makes the fight a real system with a real fail state.

02 — Data model

Where the state lives

player

health · energy · food · hope · dread · weaponId · weaponDurability · dead
The run's truth. Health is why death can exist at all — today it is a hardcoded 72 inside the shelter screen.

combat

allies[] · enemies[] · queue · round · phase · busy · log[]
Fight-scoped. Owns the timers so a queued beat can never fire after the screen is gone.

board

Tile.encounter · Tile.resolved
The world owns the encounter. cleared still means "free to re-enter"; resolved is a separate axis.

inventory

bag[5] · consumeBagSlot() · addToBag()
The bag is the only inventory that exists in the field.

data/combat

Every tuning number, in one file. Enemies, weapon, Dread, retreat odds, loot.

Allies is an array, sized 1. CLAUDE.md requires combat built for 1–4 player-side actors from the start, so there is no scalar juanHp anywhere in the fight — companions slot in without a rebuild. Juan's HP is the one field that deliberately doesn't live on his actor entry: it lives in the player store, because damage and death have to outlive the encounter.
03 — The fight, beat by beat

Turn loop

ROUND N

Queue is rebuilt from actor speeds. Juan acts first (speed 5 vs 3); ties go to the player. Queue order is visible in the header.

JUAN

One action: Attack · Guard · Use Item · Retreat. Guard expires the moment his next turn starts.

SHAMBLER

Each living enemy takes its turn in order — lunge, Juan's hurt frame, damage, Dread rises, log appends.

END

Round increments, queue rebuilds, repeat.

Resolving an attack

HIT ROLL

weapon accuracy − Shaken penalty, clamped. Miss is a real outcome with its own line.

t+180ms

Wind-up frame → swoosh frame, hit flash, damage floater. HP bar drops on impact, not on tap.

DURABILITY

−1 swing. At 0 the plank splits along the grain: damage falls to bare hands, and Retreat starts looking wise.

DREAD

+6 per swing, +10 per hit taken. ≥ 60 is Shaken.

KILL

Die frame holds for a beat, then the rank restages — the next Shambler steps up into the targetable slot.

Retreat is a real trade now. Base 25%, +15% per Shambler already put down, −10% when Shaken. Success returns you to the map with the tile still holding its zombie — walk back and you fight the same encounter again. Today, walking away costs literally nothing, which makes every fight optional.

What that produces.

One Shambler: ~2 swings, about 3 health lost. Barely a fight. Two: ~4 rounds, roughly 9 health. Three: ~5 rounds and about 17 health. Four: ~7 rounds and roughly 30 health — a real cost, not a coin flip. The curve is deliberately gentle; if fights should bite harder, the knobs are accuracy (0.7 → 0.8), the 3+ tail weights, or enemy HP — all in data/combat.ts, per enemy type.

05 — Death

Health reaching 0 anywhere ends the run — combat or not. A death screen reports how far you got (nights survived, tiles cleared, Shamblers put down) and restarts at Nida's with the meters and bag reset. The prologue does not replay: you've died, you know the story.

The never-stranded rule is untouched — it governs the movement economy (a cleared path home is always free), and was never a promise of immortality. It's the same line every survival game draws: you can always walk home, you just might not survive the walk.
06 — Assumptions I made, and what's still open
Assumed (say the word and any of these move):
  • Using an item costs your turn — it's one of the four actions.
  • Sleep's fuller role (energy, dread decay) belongs to the shelter-loop pass; for now, arriving at the shelter settles Dread into Hope and clears it. That is the hook the design doc actually asks for.
  • Retreating and re-approaching restarts the encounter at full enemy HP — the specs are rolled once and stored on the tile, but damage isn't remembered between attempts.
  • Battery-torch is the "steady your nerves" item: relieves Dread, small Hope kick.
  • Victory loot drops into the field bag, not the shelter stash — you're out in the field.
  • Searching a location tile always yields one item, rolled from a weighted table: staples common, medicine rare. One search per place — the card resolves on acknowledgement, and a searched building stays searched.
  • Enemy damage is per-archetype, so a future type brings its own curve, accuracy and HP.
Deliberately not in this pass: actual companions (only their slots), weapon switching UI, status effects beyond Shaken, and mid-fight localStorage persistence — backgrounding the app mid-fight still loses the fight, which matches the rest of the app until Phase 2 lands.
